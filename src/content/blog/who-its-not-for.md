On every guide page here there are two lists side by side. The left one says who the guide is for. The right one says who it is not for, in plain terms, above the buy button.

That is an odd thing to put next to a price, and it is deliberate.

## The arithmetic of a wrong sale

A guide sold to someone it does not suit is not a small win. It is a loss with a delay on it.

They read it, find it is not the thing they needed, and are left with a choice between asking for their money back or quietly deciding this was not worth it. Either way it cost more than the sale was worth: a refund and the time around it, or a person who will not be back and has an opinion to share.

A guide not sold to that person costs nothing at all. So the honest column is not generosity. It is the cheaper option.

## What it stops

The lists are specific because vague ones do nothing. *Not for beginners* is not information. These are the kinds of lines that are:

- Anyone looking for a template to copy without understanding it, because the guides explain why, not only how.
- Anyone expecting the tools to replace a person. They take the repetitive part; judgement stays human.
- Anyone who wants a cinematic brand film. The tools cannot deliver that polish, and pretending otherwise wastes a week.

Each one is aimed at somebody who could plausibly have clicked buy, and who would have been annoyed afterwards.

## The same logic elsewhere

It is the reason the refund policy says what is not refunded as well as what is, and the reason the guides have chapters on where the approach stops working. A guide that only tells you what is possible is a brochure. The useful part is knowing where the edges are before you have promised something to a client.

If you read the two columns and you are on the wrong side of them, do not buy it. That is what the column is there for.
